The 6 signs

  • One flattering photoOften a stolen or stock photo. Save it and reverse-image-search it before you trust it.
  • "Captain" / widower / offshoreA job and a backstory built to explain why he can never meet, call unexpectedly, or be checked up on.
  • A "Verified" badgeVerification usually just checks a selfie matches a photo it proves nothing about who someone really is.
  • Replies within minutes, any hourReal offshore workers have patchy signal. Constant availability from someone claiming isolation is a mismatch.
  • "The rig wifi is not good company"A built-in, pre-loaded excuse for why video calls never quite work.
  • "Tired of being alone too"Goes straight for loneliness. Real connection takes time; this is designed to feel instant.

What to actually do

  • Never send money, vouchers or gift cards to someone you've only met online - whatever the story.
  • Ask for a spontaneous video call. An excuse every single time is the tell.
  • Reverse-image-search their photo (Google Images - camera icon).
  • Tell a friend or family member early. Scammers work by asking you to keep it secret.
  • Trust the uneasy feeling. Moving fast and asking for money is the pattern, not the exception.
Already sent money? Call your bank immediately, then report it to Report Fraud - 0300 123 2040 or https://www.reportfraud.police.uk/. You won't be in trouble, and it helps stop them doing it to someone else.
